Your day in Slate Operations

HR works in Slate Operations. Your role decides what you see. HR sees more than an artist, on the same screens.

Signing in

  1. Open Slate OperationsThe sign-in window opens at once, even while it is still connecting.
  2. Type your User nameIt looks like priya.sharma. Then your Password.
  3. Press Sign inA brand-new account must choose its own password first: "Choose your own password to sign in."
  4. You are punched inSigning in starts your day if it has not started. Not on a day of approved full-day leave.

The HR role is called HR (or Human Resources). Admin and Developer accounts can do all of the HR work too. A change to somebody's role shows the next time they sign in.

Your own leaveAsk for it in Leave → My leave, like anybody. Nobody can decide their own request. In your queue it shows "Needs another approver", and Approve stays off. Another HR person, or an admin, has to decide it. In a one-person HR team, agree in advance who that is.

The leave queue: supervisor first, HR last

Every request needs two yeses. The supervisor says whether the team can spare the person. HR gives the final yes, and only then are the days spent.

Leave → Queue opens on Waiting on me. The list is sorted: your decisions first, then the soonest start date. Somebody leaving on Monday needs an answer before somebody leaving next month.

Waiting on you counts requests at Pending HR, withdrawals (Cancellation requested), and requests stuck with a supervisor who cannot act. Elsewhere in the chain is open requests still with a supervisor who can act. Away today is approved leave covering today; Approved this month is approved leave starting this month.

The filter offers Waiting on me, Everything outstanding, Everything, and each status on its own. The search finds a name, a reason or a type. A person who has left shows "(left)" after their name. Their request still needs a decision.

Approve

  1. Pick a requestRead the panel on the right: working days, sandwich days, balance before and after, who else is away.
  2. Press ApproveYou can select several rows first (Ctrl or Shift and click).
  3. Read the question"That is the final approval - the days will be deducted." It also names anybody who will be overdrawn.
  4. Press YesA short message says how many were recorded.

Reject, withdrawals, revoke

Every Approve and Reject rings the bell of the person who asked; a rejection carries your reason. When a supervisor approves, every active HR approver gets a bell: "... is waiting for HR." Withdrawal decisions and Revoke send no bell: tell the person yourself.

Say noSelect, press Reject, type the reason, press Reject.A reason is required. The person sees it under Decision.
Decide for an absent supervisorChange the filter to Everything outstanding. Select a Pending Supervisor row. Approve or Reject.Both stages are done at once. It is recorded as "Decided by HR on behalf of the supervisor."
Agree to a withdrawalSelect a Cancellation requested row, press Approve.The leave becomes Cancelled. The days, and any comp off, come back.
Refuse a withdrawalSelect it, press Reject, give the reason.The leave stays Approved. The note says "Withdrawal refused: ...".
Take back approved leaveSelect an Approved row, press Revoke, give the reason.The days come back. If the leave has already started, Slate warns: those days will show as absent in attendance.
Correcting a request with wrong datesThere is no button to change the dates of a request. If it is still pending, the person presses Withdraw in My leave and asks again. If it is approved and not started, they press Withdraw and you approve the withdrawal. If it has already started, the person cannot withdraw it: you Revoke it with a reason, and they ask again for the right days. Past dates are allowed; Slate marks them as a retrospective request.

Balances: what leave costs

Casual, Sick and Earned share one balance, called paid leave. Slate works out what each request costs when it is sent, and keeps that number.

How paid leave is earned

  • 2 days for every completed calendar month (the studio default). The days arrive on the last day of the month.
  • A month the person did not work all of does not count. Somebody who joined on 15 January earns nothing for January. On 30 March they have 2 days (February). On 31 March they have 4.
  • No joining date means accrual from 1 January. Users & Roles shows a missing joining date in amber. Fix it before the year end.
  • Nothing is earned after the last working day.

On My leave the person sees Paid leave available (earned, minus approved leave including leave booked for later, minus requests still waiting; it can be negative, which means overdrawn), Earned, Awaiting approval, Booked (approved, not started) and, where the studio operates it, Comp off.

Why one day off can cost three

The sandwich rule is on by default. If the working days somebody takes have a day off on both sides, those days off are charged too. Slate judges this on the working days taken, so asking for more days never costs less.

Before you approve a long requestLook at Sandwich days in the details panel. People are often surprised that a full week costs 8 days. Request leave shows them the cost before they send it, but it is worth saying once more.

The cost is fixed when the request is sent

Changing the holiday list or the policy later does not change approved leave. For requests still waiting, Slate offers to work the cost out again when you add, change or remove a holiday ("Re-cost waiting requests").

When a request is refused before it reaches you

  • It overlaps a request the person already has (pending, approved, or waiting on a withdrawal).
  • The dates hold no working days.
  • It starts before the joining date, ends after the last working day, or falls in a leave year HR has closed.
  • Paid leave beyond the balance: Request leave says "ask for Unpaid instead, or shorten it".
  • The holiday list could not be read. Nothing is saved, so holidays are never charged as leave by mistake.
Overdrawn is still possibleA balance can go below zero later, for example after a correction. When you approve, Slate names who will be overdrawn and by how much, and suggests rejecting and asking for Unpaid instead.

Comp off

Comp off is a day back for working a day off. Slate earns it from the attendance record, never by hand. It is off by default.

Turning it on

Settings → Studio policy → Comp-off: tick "Working a weekly off or a holiday earns comp-off", set the values, press Save changes at the top. Until then, nobody can ask for Comp Off and nothing is credited.

The values, with their names in Studio policy: For a weekly off worked 1 day; For a holiday worked 1 day (both only from half a standard day, 4.5 h); Half a day for working at least 12 hours and A whole day for working at least 18 hours on a normal working day; Unused comp-off lapses after 90 days.

Crediting: two ways, never twice

  1. By itselfWhile the Slate Server window is open: about 5 minutes after it opens, then once a day, from the last 90 days. It also lapses expired comp off. Skipped while the database is down.
  2. HR tools → Comp off earned...Pick Look back: 90 days, 30 days or A year. Each row: Person, Day, Hours, Earns, Why. Greyed rows at the bottom are duplicates (see below).
  3. Untick what is wrongEvery row starts ticked. Untick a day that should not count. It comes back next time: if a punch is wrong, correct the punch.
  4. Credit theseConfirm. Each day is written with its reason and expiry. A day already credited is never credited again.
"Duplicate - not counted"Before 2.2.0 a day could be credited twice. The upgrade marked the later copies as duplicates and deleted nothing. A duplicate counts in no balance, no spend and no expiry. You see them greyed and read-only at the bottom of HR tools → Comp off earned..., marked "Duplicate - not counted", so you can explain them to people.

Spending comp off

The person asks for Comp Off in Request leave. Slate shows how much comp off is valid on that date. At your final approval Slate checks again. If there is not enough, Approve is refused with a message like "Ravi Menon has only 0.5 days of comp off left for 2 Nov 2026, and this needs 1 day. Reject it and ask for Unpaid instead." The days closest to expiring are used first.

Attendance

Everybody punches in and out. HR sees the whole studio, corrects days, and exports the month for payroll.

Correcting a day

  1. Double-click the dayIn Team overview. Edit punch opens with the name and date. A day that has not happened yet cannot be edited.
  2. Pick the timesIn - out. Tick No in time or No out time if one is unknown. Add session / Remove for breaks.
  3. Overnight?Tick Ends next day (overnight shift) when the out time is after midnight. Only then may out be earlier than in.
  4. Type the reason, SaveA reason is required. The day keeps who changed it, why, when, and the old times (point at the cell).
  • A day that had no record opens at 10:00 to 19:00. Change both to the real times.
  • A day closed by the automatic punch-out says so: "Out was set by the automatic punch-out at 19:30 - enter the real time if it is known."
  • Clear day removes the day's record. It also needs a reason, kept in the audit log.
  • A day of approved leave asks before adding punches.

Supervisors see My team: the people under them, read-only. When they spot a wrong day, they ask you to correct it.

The biometric import, step by step

The biometric machine's export file (CSV or Excel) goes into the same attendance record as the Punch buttons. Any machine works.

  1. Copy the exportFrom the machine to your PC (a USB drive is fine). CSV, TXT, XLSX, XLSM or XLS.
  2. Attendance → Team overview → Import biometricThen Choose file… and pick the file.
  3. Check the column boxesOne must be Employee code. The time needs Date + time, or both Date and Time. In / Out if the file has it. Others: Ignore.
  4. Check the date orderDates are day first (31/12/2026) is ticked. Untick it only for a machine that writes the month first.
  5. Match unknown codesType a name in the person box. Leave it empty to skip that code. If the file has a Name column, Slate suggests the person. A code that is somebody's Slate user name matches by itself.
  6. Press Import, then Yes"Write 47 days of attendance for 24 people?" When it is done, Attendance says how many days were imported.

What Slate does with the punches

  • One day per person: the earliest punch is the in, the latest is the out.
  • Night shifts: when the file has an In / Out column, a late IN and the next morning's OUT (within 16 hours) become one overnight day. Without In / Out nothing is moved.
  • A day the workstation also recorded keeps the earlier in and the later out. A machine time replaces an out set by the automatic punch-out or a missing punch-out.
  • Importing the same file twice changes nothing. Days already correct are counted as "already correct".
  • Days outside employment (before joining, after the last working day) are listed and left out, unless you tick Import those days anyway.
  • Remembered for next time: the column boxes and your code matches are saved in the studio's shared folder, for that file layout. The next file from the same machine shows "Known file layout".
When something does not importShow skipped lines lists every line that could not be read, and why. If some days fail, the window stays open with a list: Person, Day, Reason. Fix the file or the record, then import again. Days already written are left alone.

Holidays and the year end

The holiday list decides what leave costs. The year end decides what carries into the next year. Both are HR's.

The holiday list

Two ways in: Leave → Queue → HR tools → Holidays..., or Attendance → Team overview → Holidays (it opens on the year you are looking at). Do the whole year in one sitting, before people book leave.

  1. Pick the YearOr All years. Past holidays are dimmed.
  2. Date and NameFor example "Diwali".
  3. Applies toAll, or one place. The place must be spelled as in people's Location.
  4. Press AddIf waiting requests cover that date, Slate asks to re-cost them. Say Yes.
  • Edit (or double-click) corrects a date, name or place in one step. Remove deletes. Both offer to re-cost waiting requests.
  • Approved leave keeps the day count it was charged at. Only waiting requests are re-costed.
  • A second holiday on the same date for the same place is refused ("Already on the calendar"), whatever the letter case.
  • A place nobody's record names asks first: "Nobody works there ... this holiday would apply to nobody."

Closing a leave year

Closing a year draws a line under it. Up to the carry-forward cap (12 days by default) moves into the new year. The rest lapses. Even before you close, balances already apply the cap to a finished year, and the queue shows a banner: "Leave year 2025 has not been closed." Closing writes the record that balances then count from.

  1. Fix joining dates firstPeople with no joining date are listed as "No joining date - set it first" and are not closed. Set the date in Users & Roles.
  2. HR tools → Year end...Leave year shows the next year to close. Read the table: nothing is written yet.
  3. Close the yearSlate says how many people and how many days lapse in total. "It cannot be undone from this screen."
  4. Read the result"2 closed, 1 were already done." Anyone not saved is named. Close again to finish them.
Rules Slate holds you toOnly a finished year can be closed ("2026 has not finished yet"). Years close in order ("Close 2025 first"). A year already closed is never closed twice for the same person. After a close, nobody can ask for leave in that year, and balances count from what was carried.

Service accounts, people who left before the year began, and people who joined after it ended are left out. A closed year can still be opened in the window to look back (marked "closed - view only"). If leave in that year changed after the close, the status says "Closed - now ..." with the new figure; balances still count from what was recorded.

Joining and leaving

One checklist, read in two directions. HR starts it and does the paperwork lines. IT does the accounts and machines. Each team sees only its own lines.

Starting somebody joining

  1. Make the account firstIn Users & Roles → Add user. The checklist needs an account to belong to.
  2. Joining & Leaving → Start joiningPerson: type a name. Text that matches nobody is refused.
  3. Employment, Department, Joining onFilled from the person's record. If a joining date is already on record, Slate shows it and asks before changing it.
  4. Press Start joiningThe person appears on the left. Their lines appear on the right.

Working the list

  • The left table: Person, Joining / leaving, Date, Your tasks, Total open. Total open counts both teams' lines, so you can see whether somebody is really ready.
  • Select lines, press Mark done. Reopen undoes it. Done by shows who ticked it and when.
  • Cancel this checklist removes the open lines of a list started by mistake. Ticked lines stay as the record. For a leaving list, Slate also asks whether to clear the last working day.
  • The filter: Everyone outstanding, Joining, Leaving, Everyone (Everyone also shows finished lists).

Starting somebody leaving

Start leaving asks for the Last working day. The picker also offers people who have already left, marked "(left)", so their kit is still chased.

A last working day in the past switches the account off at onceSlate asks first. From the day after the last working day the person cannot sign in, earns no more leave, is not marked absent, and is left out of later year ends. A last day before the joining date is refused.

If a machine is still out with somebody whose last day has passed (or who was deactivated), a red Kit not returned card appears at the top. It is there only when it is true. The heading of each list says what the person still holds ("Currently holding: ...").

Somebody coming back

A finished joining list is not reused. For a re-hire: Reactivate the account in Users & Roles (it offers to clear a last day that has passed), then Start joining. A fresh list is laid down. An open list is topped up, never doubled. A joining list cannot be started for an account that is still switched off.

People records

A person's record in Users & Roles drives leave, attendance and the dashboard. Get it right once and everything else follows.

The fields that matter to HR

FieldWhat it decides
Joined onWhen leave starts to accrue. Before it, the person is never marked absent. Missing: accrual from 1 January, and the year end skips them.
Last working dayAfter it, the account is switched off. Leave stops accruing. Clear it (Not set) for somebody coming back.

Who reports to whom

  • Reports to only offers active people who approve leave (Supervisor and Lead roles) or keep it (HR). Never a system account.
  • Somebody cannot report to themselves, and loops are refused ("... would make a loop").
  • A supervisor's My team in Attendance shows everybody under them, through their leads too. A supervisor with nobody recorded under them sees "Nobody reports to you yet".
  • The Users list marks in amber anybody whose Reports to cannot approve leave, and anybody with no joining date.

Departments

The Department field is picked from the studio's list (Matte Painting, CG, Roto, Prep / Paint, Matchmove, Comp, Slap Comp, Face / De-age, AI, Motion Graphics, Production, IT, HR, Admin, Editorial, General). It is read from the person's own record, never from what a screen sends:

  • A Lead edits only their own department on the VFX Dashboard. It comes only from the Department box on their own record. A Lead whose Department names no department can edit nothing there until you set it. A change reaches the dashboard at the lead's next sign-in.
  • The All departments filter in Team overview, and the Department suggested when you start a checklist.
  • For somebody with no manager, "Also away from the team" in the queue compares people of the same department.

Adding, importing, exporting

  1. One person: Add userUsername (2 to 40 letters, numbers, dot, dash, underscore), Display name, Department, Roles, a first Password, then the fields above.
  2. Many: Import…Download template, fill it, Choose file…. Pick a Role for everyone and a First password.
  3. Read the preview, ImportEach row says what will happen. Save report… keeps the list of what was set.
  4. Export CSVThe whole list, with status and last day, as slate_users.csv.
  • The template columns: Username, Display Name, Department, Joined, Reports To, Location, Employment, Role.
  • People already in Slate are skipped, unless you tick Update existing people (profile fields only - never passwords or roles). Then an exported list can be edited and imported back.
  • New people choose their own password the first time they sign in. Tell each one their user name and the first password.

When somebody leaves for good

  • Deactivate, do not delete. History (attendance, leave) is kept; the person disappears from lists and pickers. Show deactivated brings them into view; Reactivate switches them on again.
  • Deactivate is refused while the person has leave of their own waiting for a decision, or a machine still issued. Handle those first.
  • Deactivating a supervisor offers to move their team to a new manager ("Move N people and deactivate"). Leave waiting on them follows the team.
  • Delete… is only for an account made by mistake, with no history. You cannot deactivate or delete yourself.
  • Reset password sets a new one. Nobody can read a password, not even an admin.
