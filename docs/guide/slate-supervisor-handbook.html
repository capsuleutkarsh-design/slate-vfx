Your role in Slate

Slate comes as two programs on your PC. Slate Studio (its window title says Slate VFX) has the shot work. Slate Operations has the people work. As a supervisor or lead you need both.

Your PC has both programsSupervisor and lead PCs get both Slate Studio and Slate Operations. Leave approval, your team's attendance and the Admin Panel are only in Slate Operations; Slate Studio does not show them at all.

Your team

Slate does not guess who is in your team. It reads two things from each person's own record in Users & Roles: their Reports to and their Department. HR and admins keep those records. You cannot change them yourself.

Three rules

QuestionSlate's answer
Whose leave do I decide?People whose Reports to is you. Only one level down.
Whose attendance do I see?Everybody below you: your reports, their reports, and so on.
Which dashboard columns may a lead change?The department named in the Department box of the lead's own Users & Roles record. For example "Roto", "Prep / Paint" or "Comp". The screen and saving both read it. A change shows at the lead's next sign-in.

Reviewing shots

An artist sends a version. You look at it, write notes and give a verdict. The verdict changes the shot. The artist is told when you save.

The Review queue

  1. VFX Dashboard → Reports → Review queueVersions sent for review that have no verdict yet, the oldest first.
  2. Open in playerOr double-click a row. The window is called Review: SH010 - v003.
  3. Note, then Approve or RetakeType in Add a review note (it is signed with your name), then press a verdict. The player moves on to the next version.
  4. Save on the dashboardPress Save N changes (Ctrl+S). Now the artist is told.
Who sees the verdict buttonsOnly people who can edit the dashboard. A lead gets them only on versions of their own department. Everybody else can open the queue and play versions, but the verdict controls are hidden. Artists see only their own shots in the queue.

Review in RV

For a careful look at full frames, use RV. Right-click a shot on the dashboard and choose Review in RV…, or press Review in RV at the top of the shot panel.

  1. Tick what to look atOne layer plays it. Several load as a playlist to compare. Press Open in RV.
  2. Slate menu → a verdictApprove Current Shot or Retake Current Shot. Retake always asks for a note. It is saved on the version waiting for review.
  3. Notes on a frameSend Note on This Frame... asks "Note for this frame:". Slate says "RV: note saved on SH010 v003." A drawing on the frame goes with it.
  4. Save in SlateThe shot's new status waits on the dashboard. Press Save N changes.
Which version RV changesSlate finds the version waiting for review from the folder of the media you played, or from its version number (v003). The verdict is saved on that version, so it leaves the Review queue, exactly as a verdict given in Slate. A note alone goes on that version, or on the newest one. If Slate cannot tell which version it is, the verdict goes on the shot only, and the version stays in the queue. Without the right to edit the dashboard, nothing changes.

Leave and comp off: the first approval

You give the first answer on your team's leave. HR give the second and final one. Your question is simple: can the work spare this person on these days?

The Queue

Open Leave in Slate Operations. Approvers get two tabs: Queue and My leave (for your own leave). The Queue opens on Waiting on me: your team's requests that need your answer.

  1. Select one requestRead the Details: the dates, working days, sandwich days and the balance after.
  2. Check who else is awayAlso away from the team lists colleagues with leave on those dates, approved or still waiting.
  3. ApproveA box asks "Approve 1 request?" and says it goes to HR. Press Yes.
  4. Or Reject, with a reasonType why in "Why? The person will see this." Reject stays off until you write something.

You can select several rows and approve or reject them together. When it is done, a small note says N requests recorded.

Reading the cost of a request

You seeIt means
DaysWhat the request costs, worked out by Slate from the studio calendar. You cannot change it, and the person saw the same number before sending.
Sandwich daysThe sandwich rule is on in a new studio. If the working days taken have a day off on both sides, those days off are charged too. Example: Sunday is the weekly off and Tuesday is a holiday. Taking only Monday costs 3 days. A half day never pulls in the days around it.
Balance after in redThe person would be overdrawn. Approve warns: "Kiran P. will be overdrawn by 2 days." and suggests rejecting and asking for Unpaid instead.
Casual (first half)A half day: the first or the second half of one day. It costs 0.5.
Waiting on: HRAlready past you. The buttons stay off for it.
Waiting on: Needs another approverYour own request. Nobody approves their own leave. It goes to your manager or to HR.

Comp off

Comp off is a day back for working a weekly off, a holiday, or a very long day. It is used only if the studio has switched it on (it is off in a new studio). The person asks for leave of type Comp Off, and it comes to your Queue like any other leave. There is no separate comp-off screen, and you do not credit comp off: HR do.

StepWhoWhat happens
EarnedAttendanceBy default: a weekly off or holiday worked for at least half a standard day earns 1 day; 12 hours in a day earns half a day, 18 hours a full day. Open days, missing punches and auto-closed days never earn it.
CreditedHRHR tools → Comp off earned... HR can untick a day before crediting. Unused comp off lapses after 90 days by default.
First approvalYouThe same Queue and buttons. The Details show "Comp off: N days earned and unspent", any held by other waiting requests, and "After this request".
Final approvalHRSlate checks the comp off left for that date. If there is not enough, HR's approval is refused and Slate suggests Unpaid instead.

What HR do next

  • Final approval. HR's box says "That is the final approval - the days will be deducted." Then the status is Approved.
  • Or a rejection, with a reason, even after you approved.
  • Requests stuck with you. HR can decide a request still waiting on a supervisor. Slate records it as "Decided by HR on behalf of the supervisor."
  • No supervisor can act. If a person's Reports to is empty, is themselves, is HR, or is someone who cannot approve leave, the request goes straight to HR. HR see it marked "No supervisor can act - yours to decide".
  • Withdrawals and Revoke of approved leave are between the person and HR. You are not asked.
Who hears about a decisionThe person who asked gets a bell message for every decision: "Your Casual leave (…) was approved by you and now waits for HR.", or "…was rejected by you:" followed by your reason. When you approve, every HR approver gets a bell that the request is waiting for HR. My leave shows the same in its Status, Waiting on and Decision columns.

Scheduling and bidding

These two screens belong to production. The Supervisor and Lead roles do not have them in a new studio. If your admin adds them to your role, you read them; you do not change them.

"Read-only" at the topChanging the schedule needs Edit the schedule; making bids needs Edit bids; Won and Lost need Approve bids. By default Production Head has all three; Production Coordinator and Producer can edit the schedule and bids.

Scheduling: is my team over-booked?

Scheduling has three views of the same plan: Table, Timeline (a Gantt chart) and People. The People view is the one for you.

  • Only approved leave shows as away. A request still waiting is not shown. If it matters, decide it in your Leave Queue first.
  • Work without a target date or bid days is not drawn. The view says how many such assignments it left out.

Bidding: how is the job tracking?

Select a Won bid and look at Tracking below the list. Department by department it compares the days bid, the days planned on the dashboard, the days delivered and the Actual days recorded, with Delivered % and what remains. A department planned or spent above its bid is red. Tracking compares only what was recorded, so ask your leads to fill in each department's Actual days in the shot panel when work is approved. A dash means "not recorded".
