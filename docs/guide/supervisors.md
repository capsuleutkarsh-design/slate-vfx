# Slate, for supervisors and leads

You are the **first** of the two leave approvals, and you have the production screens.

---

## Leave: the first decision

**Leave** shows your team's requests waiting on you. Yours first, then soonest
starting — somebody leaving on Monday needs an answer before somebody leaving next
month.

Your question is not the policy question. HR handle entitlement, the calendar and the
day count; Slate has already worked all of that out and the **Days** column shows what
the request will actually cost.

Your question is simply: **can the studio spare them that week?**

Approving sends it to HR for the final decision — the dialog says so before you
confirm, because "approved" means different things at the two stages and you should
know which one you just did.

Rejecting requires a reason. The artist will read it.

You can only act on requests at your stage. A request already with HR shows in the
list with **HR** in the *Waiting on* column, and the buttons stay off.

### What the artist already saw

Before they submitted, they were shown what the request costs and why — including the
sandwich rule, which can turn one day off into three. So the number in the **Days**
column is not a surprise to them, and it is not negotiable at your end.

## Production

- **VFX Dashboard** — shots, statuses, artists and departments. Backed by the studio database, or by an Excel sheet where a show still runs that way.
- **Scheduling** — who is on what.
- **Bidding** — what a job should cost.
- **Timeline Viewer** — reel lineup.
- **Build & Ingest** — shot structure from a template, and scans moved into it.

---

## Away today

The **Away today** figure on your leave screen counts approved leave that is in
progress right now. It is the one to glance at before you promise a delivery date.
