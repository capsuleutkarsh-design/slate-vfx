Every day: signing in

  1. Turn on your PCSlate opens by itself when Windows starts. It shows the sign-in window.
  2. Type your User nameFor example priya.sharma. Slate remembers it next time and puts you straight in the Password box.
  3. Type your PasswordDots hide it. The eye shows it. Caps Lock is on warns you before you type it wrong.
  4. Press Sign inOr press Enter. Your name shows at the top right when you are in.

Your first sign-in with a new password

When your account is new, or an administrator has reset your password, Slate asks you to choose your own straight after you sign in.

  1. Choose your passwordThe window has this title. It does not ask for the old one.
  2. New password, twiceType it in New password and in New password again. At least 8 characters.
  3. Save passwordCancel takes you back to the sign-in window. You cannot go in without choosing one.

Changing your password

  1. Click your nameTop right of the window. The account menu opens.
  2. Change password…Type your Current password, then the new one twice.
  3. Save password"Password changed. Use it the next time you sign in." Use it in both Slate Studio and Slate Operations.
Nobody can read your passwordSlate keeps only a one-way lock of it. Not even an administrator can see it. If you forget it, ask your administrator to reset it; you then choose a new one at your next sign-in. Spaces at the start or end are ignored.

Signing out and closing

If a screen has changes you have not saved, or a rename is still running, Slate asks before it closes or signs you out. Closing Slate or signing out never punches you out of attendance. Starting Slate again while it is already open brings the open window to the front.

The bell

The bell beside Help collects your notifications: shots given to you, a verdict that changes your shot ("SH010 is now RETAKE."), new scans, decisions on your leave and replies to your tickets. The number is what you have not read. Double-click a notification to open what it is about: the shot, or your ticket.

The shot pipeline, as an artist sees it

Production brings the plates in and gives you the shot. You do the work and send it for review. A supervisor gives the verdict.

Where your shot lives on disk

Every shot has the same folders. This is the studio's standard template; a project can use another one, so look at your project's folders if they differ.

The shot panel

Double-click a shot, or press Enter, to open it beside the grid.

  • Preview plays the latest render in Quick Look. Space does the same from the grid.

Stock Viewer

The studio's library of footage, elements and textures: anything reused across jobs. You can look, search, play and copy. Leads and supervisors add to it.

Finding something

  • Search looks at names, tags, categories, folder names and what was read from the file: resolution (4K, 1920), frame rate (24fps), codec and kind (movie, image sequence). Each word must match the start of a word: HD finds HD clips, not UHD ones. Esc clears it.
  • Look narrows to Dark, Bright, Warm, Cold, Green Screen or Blue Screen pictures. Sort orders the whole library.
  • Favourites are yours alone: click the star on a card, press Favourite, or press Ctrl+D. Studio picks are shared with everybody and chosen by the people who can ingest stock.

CAP Rename

Rename many files at once. Slate builds a full preview first. Read the preview before you press the button.

Find and replace

  1. Add the filesAdd files…, Add folder…, or drop them on the list.
  2. Type Find and Replace withFor example _v003 and _v004. Keep extension is on, so .exr is left alone.
  3. Read the previewShow conflicts lists only problems. Remove selected takes a file out.
  4. Rename filesSlate asks once more: Rename 2 files. Then it renames them.

Number as a sequence

Every file becomes Base name + a number, in the order of the list. Fill in Base name (for example shot_010_v01_), Start frame (1001 unless you change it), Step (1) and Padding (4 digits). Set the order with Sort by name, Sort by date modified, or the up and down arrows (Custom order). If the numbers outgrow the padding, Slate offers Use 5 digits.

All or nothing, and UndoIf one file cannot be renamed, for example because it is open in Nuke or RV, no file is renamed and the Status column says which and why. Undo last rename puts the old names back exactly, even swapped or shifted names. You see and undo only your own renames; Slate keeps your newest 50. If the files were moved or renamed again since, Undo refuses and changes nothing.

Sanitize cleans names but keeps letters in any language, and keeps the dot before a frame number (plate.1001.exr stays as it is).

Attendance

In Slate Operations, which is on your PC beside Slate Studio. Punch in when you start and out when you finish. Slate shows only the next button that makes sense.

  1. Morning: sign inSigning in to Slate Operations punches you in: "Punched in at 09:40." Or press Punch in on Home or Attendance.
  2. From home: tick the boxWorking from home today on Attendance, before or after punching in. The day follows the box.
  3. Evening: Punch outSlate asks first: "Punch out at 18:42?" Closing Slate does not punch you out.
  4. Back again: Punch in againStarts a second session. The day's hours add both together.

The rules behind the buttons

  • Signing in punches you in only once a day, and only in Slate Operations. Signing in again, or after you punched out, does nothing. Slate Studio never punches you in.
  • On a day of approved full-day leave, signing in does not punch you in. If you press Punch in yourself, Slate asks: "You are on approved leave today (Casual). Punch in anyway?" If you are really working, ask HR to cancel the leave; it stays charged until they do. A half day of leave does not ask, because you work the other half.
  • Late means after the late cut-off (10:45 unless your studio changed it), and only on a day you were expected in. A first-half leave day is judged from the afternoon.
  • Short day means fewer hours than the standard day (9 hours) on a finished day.
  • In LOCAL MODE the automatic punch-in does not work. Punch in by hand when the connection is back.

Asking for leave

  1. Request leaveThe Request leave window opens.
  2. Type, From, To, ReasonDuration: Full day, First half or Second half. A half day is one date only. Add a short Reason: it is required, and your supervisor and HR both see it.
  3. Read the cost"This will cost 3 days." If it is more than you expect, the reason is written under it.
  4. Send request"Sent to Ravi Kumar - 3 days." The request now waits on your supervisor.

Why one day off can cost three: the sandwich rule

The studio works Monday to Saturday; Sunday is the weekly off. Holidays are free. But if you take off a working day that sits between two non-working days, the days off on both sides are charged too.

Leave types

TypePlain meaning
Casual, Sick, EarnedPaid days off. All three come from the one Paid leave available balance.
Comp OffA day back for a day you worked extra. Offered only if your studio uses comp-off.
UnpaidA day off without pay. No balance needed. Ask for this when your balance runs out.
Project RestRest after a hard delivery. HR grants it; you cannot ask for it. It does not come from your balance.

Who says yes

Changing your mind: Withdraw

  • Still waiting: select it and press Withdraw. "The days it is holding go back into your balance straight away." It becomes Cancelled.
  • Approved, not started yet: Withdraw asks HR to cancel it ("Sent to HR to cancel."). It shows Cancellation requested, and the days stay deducted until HR agree. If HR refuse, it goes back to Approved.
  • Already started: you cannot withdraw it. Ask HR to correct it.
"Request not sent. Nothing has been deducted."The box says why. Usually: "You already have a request covering those days" (withdraw that one first, or pick other dates); the dates are before your joining date or in a year HR closed; or "That is 5 days and you have 3 available" (ask for Unpaid instead, or shorten it). If it says your holidays or requests "could not be read", wait a moment and try again; if it keeps happening, tell IT.

Past dates are allowed. Slate marks them "retrospective" so your approver knows.

Comp-off: only where the studio uses it

Comp-off is switched off in Slate unless your studio turns it on. If it is on, you see the Comp off card and the Comp Off leave type. Look at Settings → Studio policy for your studio's rules. With Slate's starting values:

You workedYou earn
A weekly off or a public holiday, at least half a standard day (4.5 hours)1 day
A working day of 12 hours or moreHalf a day
A working day of 18 hours or more1 day
  • It is counted from your attendance, not from a form. Slate Server credits it once a day. A day is never credited twice.
  • A day still open, missing a punch, or closed by the automatic punch-out earns nothing. So punch in and out properly.
  • Unused comp-off lapses 90 days after the day you earned it.
  • Spend it by asking for Comp Off leave. If the leave is cancelled later, the comp-off days come back.

Following your ticket

  • Your open tickets are listed. Tick Include closed for old ones. A ticket in bold has a reply you have not opened.
  • The With column names who has it, or says Not yet picked up. Point at a row to see "Reply expected by …" or "Fix expected by …".
  • Select it and press Open (or double-click) to read the conversation. Type, then Send reply or Ctrl+Enter.
  • The bell tells you when IT pick it up, reply, need your answer, change its priority or resolve it.
  • A reply on a resolved ticket (a thank-you, say) goes to IT and leaves it resolved. To say it is still broken, press Still broken - reopen.
