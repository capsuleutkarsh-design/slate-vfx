# Slate, for artists

Everything here is on your side of the software. You will not see the approval
queues or the machine inventory — those belong to HR and IT, and Slate does not show
you screens you cannot act on.

---

## Asking for leave

Open **Leave**. Four figures across the top:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Available** | what you can actually take right now |
| **Accrued** | what you have earned since you joined — two days for every completed month |
| **Awaiting approval** | days held against your balance by requests nobody has decided yet |
| **Comp off** | earned by working a weekly off or a very long day, where the studio operates it |

**Available** already has your pending requests subtracted. That is deliberate: if you
ask for five days and then ask for five more before the first is decided, the second
request is measured against what would be left.

### Why a day off sometimes costs three

Press **Request leave** and the dialog shows the cost *before* you commit.

The studio works Monday to Saturday; Sunday is the weekly off. Public holidays are
free. But if you take a working day that sits between two non-working days and do not
come in, the whole block is charged — that is the sandwich rule.

> Monday is a holiday. Tuesday is a working day. Wednesday is a holiday.
> Take Tuesday off and you are charged **three days**, not one.

The dialog spells this out as you pick the dates. If the number surprises you, the
reason is written underneath it.

A half day costs 0.5.

### What happens next

Your request goes to your **supervisor** first. If they approve, it goes to **HR**.
It is only deducted once both have said yes.

The **Waiting on** column tells you whose desk it is on. If it is rejected you get the
reason — Slate will not let anyone reject a request without writing one.

## Raising a ticket with IT

Open **Tickets** → **Raise a ticket**.

Two questions decide how fast it gets picked up, so answer them honestly rather than
strategically:

**Who is affected?**

- *The whole studio, or a service everyone depends on*
- *My department, or several people*
- *Only me*

**Can you carry on?**

- *I cannot work at all — there is no way around it*
- *I have a workaround, but it is costing me real time*
- *I can carry on. This can wait.*

Slate works the priority out from those two answers. You do not pick P1 — which means
nobody has to decide whether their thing is "really" a P1, and the queue stays honest.

### Afterwards

The ticket shows who picked it up, or **Not yet picked up** if nobody has. Open it to
read the conversation and reply. When IT need something from you the ticket moves to
**Waiting on you** — that one is worth watching, because the clock is paused on their
side and running on yours.

## Attendance

Punch in when you start and out when you finish. If your studio operates comp-off,
this record is what earns it — a Sunday worked, a public holiday worked, or a shift
long enough to qualify. It is credited from the punch data, not from a form, and it
is never credited twice for the same day.

A shift that crosses midnight counts properly. That is usually the shift that earned
the comp-off in the first place.

---

## Things worth knowing

- **The balance is real.** It is not a flat annual figure; it accrues, and it accrues from your actual joining date. A month completes on your joining day, not at the end of the calendar month.
- **What a request cost is frozen when you submit it.** If the studio changes a holiday later, it cannot retroactively change what you were already deducted.
- **Nothing you see here is shared with other artists.** Your balance, your tickets and your reasons are yours.
